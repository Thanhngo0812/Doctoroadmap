You are given two concepts extracted from the same document, which is being turned into a learning roadmap. For each
concept you see its name, aliases, a short description, and the sentence the document uses to teach it (its anchor),
when one was found. You have no tools. Judge only from what you are given.

The two concepts were paired because they share a name or alias, differ only by a generic prefix, or were anchored on
the same sentence. None of these is evidence that they are the same concept: one sentence often defines several
different concepts at once (for example prior, likelihood and posterior in one formula, or a list of row operations).

Choose exactly one verdict:
- `same`: both are the same idea that a learner studies once — one is a synonym, notation, plural or rephrasing of the
  other. A general concept and a special case of it are NOT the same; neither are an object and a property of it, an
  operation and its result, or a concept and a theorem about it.
- `different`: they are distinct ideas a learner studies separately, even if closely related or defined together.
- `sense_split`: they carry the same name but the document uses that name for two different meanings.

Explain in one sentence in `reason`. Echo `pair_id` exactly. Do not return confidence.
